BASE  (9 steps, applied in this order)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. unicode_nfc
      One Ge'ez character can be encoded as different code-point sequences. NFC
      gives each a single form, so text that looks identical compares equal.

  2. strip_zero_width
      Zero-width characters carry no text and would otherwise count as token
      content.

  3. strip_bracket_tags
      The closed tag set ([unclear:SS.S], [overlap:SS.S], [laughter], [cough],
      [noise]) marks non-speech and unscoreable regions. Tags are removed in
      both modes.

  4. mark_fragments
      A trailing hyphen marks a word the speaker began and did not finish. The
      marker is read before punctuation removal deletes it. Fluent mode then
      drops the token and verbatim mode keeps it without the hyphen.

  5. expand_abbreviations
      A reader of 2015 ዓ.ም says ዓመተ ምህረት, and of እ.ኤ.አ says እንደ ኤውሮፓውያን አቆጣጠር.
      References record the spoken words and systems write either form, so both
      sides are rewritten to the spoken form before numerals and punctuation.
      The table lists each abbreviation's written forms (dotted, slashed,
      dotless, spaced) and the spelling of its expansion in edited Ethiopian
      news text. Ambiguous abbreviations (ጠ/ሚ, ሚ/ር, አ.አ) are left as written.
      Where the speaker read out the letters, the reference keeps the
      abbreviation and both sides expand it alike.

  6. numerals
      Each number span in a reference records the words spoken and their digit
      form, paired by listening to the audio. The reference is not rewritten. A
      hypothesis in words must match those words: ሁለት ከሰላሳ against ሁለት ሰዓት ተኩል
      is substitutions. A hypothesis in digits matches only the span's recorded
      digit form, which is rewritten to the span's words before punctuation
      removal. Any other digit token stays as written and misses. No parser
      reads either side. to_digits rewrites both sides to a value with a parser,
      for sets without recorded spans. --numerals overrides the direction and
      every result records it. The number of spans a system wrote as digits is
      reported with its score.

  7. remove_punctuation
      Punctuation is not acoustic content, and systems differ in whether they
      emit it. Marks become word boundaries. Inside a number a decimal point is
      kept and a digit-group comma removed, so 10,693 stays one token.

  8. casefold
      Ge'ez has no case, so this applies to embedded Latin only. A capitalized
      English word inside Amharic is not an error.

  9. collapse_whitespace
      Tokens are split on whitespace. Repeated or trailing whitespace would
      otherwise produce empty tokens.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FLUENT  (2 steps, applied in this order)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. remove_fillers
      The inventory's fillers are removed from both sides, so a system that
      omits them is not counted wrong.

  2. remove_fragments
      Unfinished words are removed from the reference only. Hypothesis tokens
      are scored as written.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VERBATIM  (2 steps, applied in this order)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. unmark_fragments
      A fragment is kept without its marker, so a system that transcribes the
      partial word matches.

  2. filler_equivalence
      Fillers are equivalent within one language class: እም for እ is correct,
      English um for Amharic እ is not.

WER-N EQUIVALENCE LAYER  (8 sets, 46 folds)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  h-series
      members  ሀ ሐ ኀ
      UNFOLDED ሓ  (declared and deliberately not folded)
      All three are [h] in modern Amharic; spelling follows etymology. ሓ is left
      unfolded: it is not an Amharic spelling of ሃ, ሀ, ሐ, ኻ or ኃ.

  kha-series
      members  ሁ ኹ
      orders   ሁሂሃሄህሆ  ኹኺኻኼኽኾ
      UNFOLDED ኻ  (declared and deliberately not folded)
      Orders 2, 3, 5, 6 and 7 of the ኸ series fold to the ሀ series. ኸ stays
      distinct, as in published Amharic normalizers. ኻ stays distinct too: it is
      rare in written Amharic, mostly /x/ in transliterated names (ኻርቱም, ኻሜኒ) or
      Tigrinya, and absent from the frozen references.

  ha-a-order
      members  ሀ ሃ
      orders   ሀ  ሃ
      ሃ and ሀ are written interchangeably for /ha/. With the h-series, ኃ reaches
      ሀ through ሃ; ሓ and ኻ do not.

  labialized-go
      members  ጎ ጐ
      orders   ጎ  ጐ
      ጐ is written for ጎ (ጐረቤት, ጎረቤት). ጓ is not folded.

  labialized-ko-qo-ho
      members  ቆ ቈ
      orders   ቆኮሆ  ቈኰኈ
      EthioBench's extension of ጐ→ጎ: ቈ, ኰ and ኈ are written for ቆ, ኮ and ሆ in
      the same way.

  s-series
      members  ሰ ሠ
      Both are [s]. ሠ is kept by etymology (ሥራ "work").

  ts-series
      members  ጸ ፀ
      One ejective sibilant. Which letter a word takes varies by region and
      schooling.

  glottal-series
      members  አ ዐ
      orders   አኡኢአኤእኦ  ዐዑዒዓዔዕዖ
      Both are [ʔ]; ዐ is mainly religious and literary. The orders are listed
      because ዓ folds straight to አ. ኣ is not an Amharic spelling and is left
      unfolded. ኧ is not folded.

LOANWORD LAYER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   step     normalize_loanwords
   table    amh_loanwords.tsv
   profiles ['lenient', 'strict']
      A loanword has no settled fidel spelling, so spellings of one word are
      folded on both sides. lenient accepts every spelling the pattern
      generates; strict accepts only those established in edited Ethiopian news
      text. Both are reported.
